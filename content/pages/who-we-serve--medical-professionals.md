## Financial challenges unique to medical and dental practices

Running a practice comes with money problems your restaurant-owning neighbor down the street never has to think about. A few show up on almost every new client's desk in the first meeting:

- High personal income tax exposure from W-2 wages, K-1 practice income, or a mix of both, often pushing physicians and dentists into the top federal brackets
- Entity structuring decisions between an S-corp, PLLC, or partnership that affect self-employment tax, liability, and how profits get paid out to owners
- Equipment financing for imaging machines, dental chairs, or surgical tools that needs to be planned around depreciation rules, not just monthly payments
- Payroll complexity across hygienists, nurses, front-office staff, and associate providers, each with different pay structures and benefit elections
- Cash flow gaps created by insurance reimbursement timelines that can run 30, 60, or 90 days behind the date of service

Most of these problems don't get solved by a bookkeeper who logs in once a quarter. They get solved by an accounting team that understands practice economics year-round.

## Proactive tax planning for high-income healthcare providers

Most tax preparers show up in March, run the numbers on what already happened, and file the return. That approach costs high earners real money. Physicians and dentists routinely leave deductions, retirement contributions, and entity elections on the table simply because nobody looked at their situation before December 31.

Shawn T. Gubler's Certified Tax Coach (CTC) credential exists for exactly this problem. Advanced tax planning for medical professionals means reviewing entity structure for practice ownership, evaluating defined benefit and cash balance retirement plans that can shelter six figures of income, timing equipment purchases against Section 179 and bonus depreciation, and building a strategy months before filing season instead of scrambling during it. Kinexus runs this planning year-round, not as a one-time engagement, through the same [advanced tax planning](/services/advanced-tax-planning) and [entity structuring](/services/entity-structuring) work applied to other high-income clients.

## CFO-level guidance for practice growth and multi-location expansion

Adding a partner, opening a second location, or bringing on an associate provider changes the financial picture of a practice in ways a standard bookkeeper isn't equipped to advise on. These decisions carry tax consequences, cash flow demands, and staffing questions that need someone reading the numbers strategically, not just recording them.

Kinexus offers [Business Management Services](/what-we-do/business-management-services): CFO-style advisory delivered by CPAs with MAcc-level training, including Chase Gubler and Shawn T. Gubler. That means help modeling the numbers on a second location, structuring a buy-in for a new partner, or setting profit distribution terms before the paperwork gets signed, not after.

## Audit protection and IRS representation for medical practices

High-income filers draw more attention from the IRS, and a physician or dentist with practice income, retirement contributions, and multiple deductions on the return is a common audit target. An IRS notice showing up in the mail is not something to handle alone or hand to whoever answers the phone at a call center.

Enrolled Agents Andrew Stewart and Peter Williams are federally licensed to represent taxpayers directly before the IRS. [Audit Protection](/what-we-do/audit-protection) covers notices, letters, and full audits at the same level of service used for the tax planning itself, so a practice facing IRS correspondence has someone who already knows the return handling it. For more complex resolution matters, [IRS Representation & Tax Resolution](/services/irs-representation-tax-resolution) picks up where a simple notice response leaves off.
