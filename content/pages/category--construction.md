## Why construction businesses need industry-specific financial guidance

A general ledger looks the same for a dentist and a drywall contractor, but the money doesn't behave the same way. Contractors deal with percentage-of-completion accounting, retainage held back on every draw, equipment that depreciates on its own schedule, and multi-entity structures built for bonding capacity. A CPA who hasn't worked a WIP schedule or reconciled a job cost report against a draw request will miss the details that actually affect your tax bill and your bank line.

This article category exists for that reason. Shawn T. Gubler, CPA, CTC, MAcc, and the Kinexus team write about the specific problems contractors and builders bring to the table, not generic small-business tax tips repackaged for a construction audience.

## Topics we cover for contractors and builders

The articles in this category track the questions we actually get asked by general contractors, subcontractors, and developers across Washington County. Expect coverage on:

## Local context for Southern Utah's construction boom

St. George isn't growing quietly. The U.S. Census Bureau has named the St. George metro area one of the fastest-growing in the country multiple times over the past decade, and the pace shows in projects like the George Washington Boulevard bridge extension connecting St. George to Washington City, underway since February 2026 to ease traffic between the two cities.

That kind of growth means more bid opportunities, but also more contractors competing for labor, materials, and bonding capacity at the same time. A contractor scaling from one crew to three needs different tax planning, entity structure, and cash flow discipline than one running a single job at a time. We write with that trajectory in mind because it's the one most of our construction clients in Washington County are on.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Construction articles

**Q: Why does construction accounting need a specialized CPA?**
A: Construction involves percentage-of-completion accounting, retainage, and job costing that generic bookkeeping doesn't track correctly. A CPA familiar with WIP schedules and bonding requirements catches issues that affect your taxable income and your ability to secure financing before they become problems.

**Q: What is a WIP schedule and why does my bank ask for one?**
A: A work-in-progress (WIP) schedule shows the status of every active job compared to billed and earned revenue. Lenders and bonding agents use it to gauge whether a contractor is over- or under-billed on projects, which affects lending decisions and bonding capacity.

**Q: How does retainage affect my construction business taxes?**
A: Retainage withheld by a general contractor or owner, typically 5 to 10 percent of each draw, may still count as taxable income depending on your accounting method, even though you haven't received the cash. Planning around this timing gap protects cash flow.
