## Why high income business owners need more than tax prep

Most CPA firms in St. George file your return once a year, tell you the damage, and see you again in twelve months. By the time you're sitting across the table in March, the tax year is closed. There's nothing left to plan around, only numbers left to report.

High income owners don't have that luxury. A construction company clearing seven figures, a physician with a growing practice, a real estate investor closing three properties a year: these situations generate tax consequences all year long, not just at filing time. Reactive prep catches what already happened. Advanced planning changes what happens next.

Shawn T. Gubler, CPA, CTC, MAcc, leads Kinexus's advanced planning work with a Certified Tax Coach credential most local firms don't carry. Combined with a Master of Accountancy-level bench that includes Chase Gubler, CPA, MAcc and RJ Mathews, CPA, the firm builds strategy around your entities and income, not a generic checklist applied to every client in St. George.

## Advanced strategies built for growing and multi-entity businesses

Owners with more than one entity, multiple properties, or a mix of active and passive income face decisions a standard 1040 preparer isn't trained to catch. Kinexus builds strategy around the structure you actually have.

### Entity structuring and restructuring
icon: Building2

S-corp elections, multi-entity holding structures, and management company setups can shift how much of your income is subject to self-employment tax. We review your entity map at least annually as revenue and holdings change.

### Income shifting and timing strategies
icon: TrendingUp

Moving income and deductions between years, entities, or family members, done correctly and documented, can lower your effective rate without triggering IRS scrutiny.

### Retirement and deferred compensation planning
icon: PiggyBank

Defined benefit plans, cash balance plans, and solo 401(k) strategies let high-earning owners defer six figures in some years while building long-term wealth outside the business.

### Multi-entity coordination
icon: FileText

When a single owner runs a construction company, a real estate holding entity, and a management company, the tax return for one affects the others. MAcc-level review catches interactions a generalist misses.

## Certified Tax Coach-level planning for high-income owners

The Certified Tax Coach designation Shawn Gubler holds requires ongoing training in strategies most CPAs never study: cost segregation studies, advanced entity elections, and timing strategies that hold up under IRS review because they're built on real transactions and real documentation.

Cost segregation, for example, reclassifies parts of a commercial or rental property into shorter depreciation schedules, front-loading deductions in years when you need them most. Entity elections like S-corp status or partnership restructuring can reduce self-employment tax exposure by tens of thousands of dollars a year for the right owner. Timing strategies around equipment purchases, retirement contributions, and income recognition move tax liability to the year where it costs you the least.

None of this is aggressive for the sake of aggressive. It's compliant, documented, and specific to owners in the higher brackets, which is exactly why a once-a-year filing firm rarely gets there. They don't have the bandwidth, and often not the credential, to build it.

## Industry-specific tax strategy for your business

A generic tax plan misses the details that actually move the needle in your industry. Kinexus builds planning around how your business actually operates.

### Contractors and construction owners
icon: Hammer

Job costing, retainage timing, and equipment depreciation decisions all carry tax weight. Planning around WIP schedules and percentage-of-completion accounting keeps your tax position aligned with how revenue actually recognizes.

### Medical professionals
icon: Stethoscope

Practice owners face entity decisions, equipment purchases, and retirement plan design questions that differ from a typical small business. We plan around practice growth, not just this year's income.

### Real estate investors
icon: Home

Cost segregation, 1031 exchanges, and passive activity rules shape what an investor owes each year. Planning ahead of a sale or acquisition, not after closing, is what protects the deduction.

### Service-based businesses
icon: Briefcase

Consultants, agencies, coaches, and staffing firms often carry uneven income and multiple contractor relationships. Entity structure and quarterly estimates get built around that variability.

Higher income draws more attention. Owners in the top brackets, especially those using entity structures and cost segregation, see more audit scrutiny than the average filer. That's not a reason to avoid the strategy. It's a reason to have representation built into the relationship from the start.

Kinexus's audit protection service puts Enrolled Agents Andrew Stewart, EA, and Peter Williams, EA, MAcc, in your corner if the IRS sends a notice, a letter, or opens an audit. ## What proactive planning looks like throughout the year

Advanced planning only works if it happens on a schedule, not once when the return is due.

### Quarterly check-ins and projections
We run updated tax projections each quarter based on your actual numbers, so estimated payments and strategy decisions reflect where the year is really heading, not last year's guess.

### Mid-year strategy adjustments
Equipment purchases, entity elections, and retirement contributions get evaluated while there's still time to act, not discovered in April when the window has closed.

### Secure digital collaboration
Documents, projections, and plans move through a secure digital portal, so you can review numbers and sign off from a job site, a clinic, or an investment property without a trip to our office.

### Year-end planning meeting
Before December 31, we sit down to finalize decisions, purchases, and contributions while there's still runway to make them count for the current tax year.
