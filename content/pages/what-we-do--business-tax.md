## Business tax planning built for St. George's growing companies

Running a construction company, a restaurant, a medical practice, or a service business in Washington County means the tax code treats you differently depending on what you do and how you're structured. A once-a-year meeting with your CPA in March doesn't catch the deductions available to a growing S-corp, the depreciation options on new equipment, or the quarterly estimated payments that keep the IRS off your back. Kinexus CPAs & Advisors treats business tax as a year-round conversation, not a spring deadline. Shawn T. Gubler, CPA, CTC, MAcc, leads tax strategy for clients across construction, restaurants, real estate, and healthcare, building plans around entity type, growth stage, and the rules specific to each industry rather than a generic checklist. St. George business owners get a team that tracks tax law changes as they happen and applies them before the deadline, not after.

## Proactive tax strategy versus once-a-year filing

Reactive tax prep looks backward: gather receipts, hand them to a preparer, hope for the best. Proactive planning looks forward. Shawn Gubler holds the Certified Tax Coach (CTC) designation, a credential built specifically around advanced strategy for high-income business owners, and it shapes how Kinexus approaches every engagement. Instead of a single filing appointment, clients get quarterly strategy sessions that revisit income projections, entity elections, retirement contributions, and deduction timing while there's still time to act. A contractor closing a strong third quarter can adjust equipment purchases before December 31. A restaurant owner expanding to a second location can restructure before the new entity opens, not after. That's the difference between a CPA who files your return and one who helps decide what the return should say. Business owners who wait until January to think about taxes have already missed most of their options for the year.

## Tax solutions tailored to your industry

Tax strategy that works for a dental practice rarely works for a general contractor. Kinexus builds plans around the rules that actually apply to your business, not a template pulled off the shelf.

### Construction
icon: Hammer

Job costing, equipment depreciation, and percentage-of-completion accounting all affect what a contractor owes and when. We plan around WIP schedules and Section 179 elections so tax timing matches cash flow instead of the calendar.

### Restaurants
icon: Coins

Tip credit calculations, multi-location payroll tax, and equipment upgrades shift a restaurant's tax picture fast. We structure entities and time major purchases around the margins restaurant operators actually work with.

### Real Estate
icon: Home

Cost segregation studies, 1031 exchanges, and passive activity rules can move a rental portfolio's tax bill by tens of thousands of dollars. We map out the tax consequences of a sale or acquisition before you sign anything.

### Medical Professionals
icon: Stethoscope

Physicians and dentists face high effective tax rates without the right entity structure and retirement plan design. We build strategy around practice ownership, equipment purchases, and multi-provider partnerships.

### Service-Based Businesses
icon: Briefcase

Consultants, agencies, and staffing firms scale fast and often outgrow their entity structure within a year or two. We revisit S-corp elections, owner compensation, and deduction strategy as revenue climbs.

## Entity structuring and multi-entity tax strategy

Growing past a single LLC often means growing into a mess: multiple businesses, real estate held personally, no holding company, and a tax bill that reflects the disorganization. Chase Gubler, CPA, MAcc, works with business owners on entity structuring that separates operating risk from real estate and investment assets, sets up holding companies where they make sense, and coordinates tax treatment across related entities. This matters most for contractors who own equipment through a separate entity, medical practices splitting the practice from the real estate, and entrepreneurs running two or three ventures under one roof. The right structure isn't a one-time decision. As revenue grows, entities get acquired, or ownership changes, the structure that worked at $500,000 in revenue may start costing money at $2 million.

Business tax planning done right lowers audit risk from the start. When a notice arrives anyway, you're not handling it alone or starting over with a new firm.
