## Why proactive planning beats year-end scrambling

Most of what ends up in a January scramble was visible back in July. A revenue jump, a new hire, an equipment purchase, a rental property closing, each one shifts a tax picture that gets expensive to fix after December 31. Shawn T. Gubler, CPA, CTC, MAcc, built the firm's advisory model around quarterly check-ins instead of a single annual meeting, because tax law rewards decisions made before the year closes, not after. Chase Gubler, CPA, MAcc, and Morgan Nethercott, CPA, work the same way with clients across construction, restaurants, real estate, and professional services: watch the numbers monthly, flag what matters, and put it in front of the client while there's still time to act. A return filed in April tells you what already happened. A conversation held in September changes what happens next. That gap between preparation and planning is where most of the savings live.
