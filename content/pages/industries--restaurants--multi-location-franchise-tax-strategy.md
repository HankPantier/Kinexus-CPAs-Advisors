## Growing beyond one location creates tax complexity most CPAs miss

One restaurant is complicated enough. Add a second location, a franchise agreement, or a holding company structure, and the tax picture changes completely. A single-location bookkeeper who tracks receipts and files one return a year has no framework for consolidated reporting across entities, sales tax registrations in multiple counties, or the payroll tax nexus that comes with opening a unit in a new jurisdiction.

Most CPA firms treat multi-unit restaurant groups like a bigger version of a single restaurant. They aren't. Franchisees and multi-unit operators need entity structures that separate liability by location, tax planning that accounts for royalty payments and build-out depreciation across every site, and a firm that reviews the numbers quarterly instead of scrambling every April. [Kinexus CPAs](/who-we-serve/restaurants) builds that structure and stays engaged with it year-round, not just at filing time.

## Tax strategy built for franchise and multi-unit restaurant groups

Franchise and multi-unit restaurant tax planning covers more ground than a single P&L. Here's what we build for growing restaurant groups.

### Entity Structuring for Growth
icon: Building2

Holding company and operating LLC structures separate real estate, equipment, and each location's liability while keeping ownership and tax reporting clean as you add units. See our [business tax](/what-we-do/business-tax) services for how this fits into your overall filing.

### Consolidated vs Separate Filings
icon: FileText

We determine whether a consolidated return or separate entity filings save more tax based on your ownership percentages, state footprint, and franchise agreement terms.

### Franchise Royalty and Fee Deductions
icon: Receipt

Royalty payments, marketing fund contributions, and franchise fees are deductible, but timing and classification matter. We make sure every dollar is captured correctly.

### Depreciation and Cost Segregation
icon: Calculator

Cost segregation studies on new build-outs and remodels accelerate depreciation on kitchen equipment, fixtures, and leasehold improvements, freeing up cash for the next location.

## Payroll and labor cost accounting across multiple sites

Payroll gets harder with every location you add. Tip credit rules, multi-state employer accounts, and labor cost benchmarks that vary by market all have to be tracked separately, then rolled up so you can see the whole picture. Our [payroll](/what-we-do/payroll) team handles the detail work so labor cost stops being a mystery.

- Tip credit compliance tracked by location and jurisdiction
- Multi-state and multi-jurisdiction employer tax accounts set up and maintained
- Labor cost benchmarking against sales, unit by unit
- Consolidated payroll accounting that ties labor cost to margin across the group

## Frequently Asked Questions About Multi location and franchise restaurant tax strategy

**Q: Do I need a different CPA for a multi-location restaurant than a single-unit restaurant?**
A: Yes. Multi-unit groups need entity structuring across locations, consolidated reporting, multi-jurisdiction payroll compliance, and unit-level profitability tracking that a general small-business bookkeeper typically doesn't handle. Kinexus CPAs builds this structure specifically for franchise and multi-location restaurant clients.

**Q: How does entity structuring work for a franchise with multiple locations?**
A: Most multi-unit franchisees benefit from a holding company structure with separate operating LLCs per location, isolating liability while simplifying consolidated tax reporting. Kinexus evaluates ownership percentages and state footprint to determine the right structure for your group.

**Q: Can franchise fees and royalty payments be deducted?**
A: Franchise fees, royalty payments, and marketing fund contributions are generally deductible, but timing and classification affect how much benefit you capture. Kinexus reviews these deductions as part of ongoing franchise tax planning, not just at year-end.

**Q: What makes multi-location restaurant payroll more complicated?**
A: Tip credit compliance, multi-state employer tax accounts, and labor cost benchmarking all vary by location, requiring consolidated payroll accounting to see true margin performance. Kinexus manages this across every site in the group.

**Q: What happens if my restaurant group gets an IRS audit notice?**
A: Andy Stewart, EA, and Peter Williams, EA, MAcc, represent Kinexus clients directly with the IRS on payroll, tip reporting, and sales tax issues common to multi-location restaurants. Audit protection is included in how the firm works with restaurant groups, not billed as a separate emergency service.
