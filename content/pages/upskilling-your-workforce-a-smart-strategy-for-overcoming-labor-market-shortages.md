## Why labor shortages are hitting St George businesses hard

Washington County's population growth has been good for business and brutal for hiring. Contractors in St George are competing with Las Vegas and Salt Lake wages for the same pool of skilled framers and electricians. Restaurant owners along St George Boulevard are running short-staffed on weekend shifts. Medical practices in the Dixie Regional corridor are waiting months to fill front-desk and clinical support roles.

Every open position costs money whether it's filled or not. Overtime climbs, service slows, quality slips, and owners end up doing the work of three people instead of running the business. A construction crew missing two skilled workers can push a project timeline by weeks. A restaurant short two line cooks turns away covers on a Friday night. The math is the same across industries: unfilled roles are a direct hit to revenue, not just an HR headache.

## The real cost of hiring new staff versus training the team you have

Recruiting a new employee typically runs 20 to 30 percent of that role's annual salary once you account for job postings, interview time, onboarding, and the productivity dip while they ramp up. For a $50,000 position, that's $10,000 to $15,000 before the new hire has produced a single billable hour or completed job.

Training an existing employee into a higher-skill role costs a fraction of that, and you're building on someone who already knows your processes, your customers, and your standards. A restaurant that cross-trains a server into a shift lead avoids an external management search entirely. A construction firm that pays for a foreman's OSHA 30 certification gets a more capable jobsite supervisor without a six-week vacancy.

This is a financial decision as much as an operational one. Shawn T. Gubler, CPA, CTC, MAcc, and the team at Kinexus work with St George business owners to model both paths against actual payroll and revenue numbers, not general industry averages, before recommending which one makes sense.

## Tax incentives and credits that make training more affordable

Training budgets go further when they're structured with tax strategy in mind. Several federal and state provisions can offset the cost of upskilling employees, and most business owners never claim them because no one flagged the opportunity during tax prep.

- Work Opportunity Tax Credit (WOTC) for hiring and training individuals from targeted groups
- Section 162 deductions for ordinary and necessary training and education expenses
- Utah workforce development incentives tied to industry-specific training partnerships
- Employer-provided educational assistance programs under Section 127, up to $5,250 per employee tax-free
- Apprenticeship and certification reimbursement structured to maximize deductibility

A year-round tax planning relationship, rather than a once-a-year filing appointment, is what catches these before December 31. Peter Williams, EA, MAcc, and Andrew (Andy) James Stewart, EA, regularly review training and hiring plans mid-year with clients specifically to identify credits like these before the window closes.

## Building an upskilling plan for your industry

A generic training budget rarely produces a return. The skills worth investing in look different depending on what you build, serve, or provide.

### Construction
icon: Hammer

Cross-train laborers into equipment operators, invest in OSHA and safety certifications, and develop a project superintendent from within instead of recruiting one externally. Pairing this with accurate WIP schedules helps you see whether a trained crew is actually improving job margins.

### Restaurants
icon: Wrench

Move line cooks toward sous chef responsibilities, train servers into shift leads, and formalize food safety certifications across the team. Multi-location operators can standardize this training once and apply it at every site.

### Medical practices
icon: Stethoscope

Certify medical assistants for expanded clinical duties, train front-office staff in billing and coding accuracy, and reduce reliance on costly locum or temp-agency staffing by building depth on your existing team.

### Service-based businesses
icon: Users

Consultants, agencies, and staffing firms grow fastest when senior staff train junior hires into billable roles faster. Formal mentorship structures and certification reimbursement cut ramp-up time and improve utilization rates.

## How proactive financial planning supports workforce investment

Upskilling only pays off if you can measure it. That means budgeting training costs against expected productivity gains, tracking whether the investment actually reduced turnover or overtime, and adjusting the plan before it drains cash flow.

This is where CFO-level insight matters more than a spreadsheet. RJ Mathews, CPA, and Chase Gubler, CPA, MAcc, work with clients throughout the year to build training costs into cash flow forecasts and quarterly reviews, not just the annual budget. Morgan Nethercott, CPA, brings the same discipline to entity-level financial statements so owners can see, industry by industry, whether a training dollar spent on a construction foreman or a restaurant shift lead is actually showing up in the numbers three months later.

A modern, secure digital process for document handling means this data is available in real time, not buried in a shoebox of receipts at tax season.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Upskilling your workforce a smart strategy for overcoming labor market shortages

**Q: Is it cheaper to train current employees or hire new staff in St George, UT?**
A: Training existing employees is typically far less expensive than recruiting new hires, which often costs 20 to 30 percent of a position's annual salary once you factor in job postings, interviews, and onboarding time. Existing staff also ramp up faster since they already know your operations.

**Q: What tax credits are available for employee training programs?**
A: Employers may qualify for the Work Opportunity Tax Credit, Section 162 deductions for training expenses, and Section 127 educational assistance programs covering up to $5,250 per employee tax-free. Utah also offers workforce development incentives tied to industry training partnerships.

**Q: How can construction companies address labor shortages through upskilling?**
A: Construction firms can cross-train laborers into equipment operators, fund OSHA safety certifications, and develop project superintendents internally rather than recruiting externally. Combining this with accurate job costing helps confirm whether trained crews are improving project margins.

**Q: How does Kinexus CPAs help businesses plan for workforce training costs?**
A: Kinexus builds training expenses into quarterly cash flow forecasts and reviews credit eligibility throughout the year rather than only at tax time. This year-round approach, led by CPAs and enrolled agents on staff, helps owners measure whether training investments are actually improving productivity.
