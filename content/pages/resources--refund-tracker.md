## Check your refund status in seconds

You don't need an account, a password, or a phone call to find out where your refund stands. Below are direct links to the official IRS and Utah State Tax Commission tools, so you can check in under a minute, no login required. We built this page because clients ask us this question every February through April, and the fastest answer is the government site itself, not a guess from us. Kinexus CPAs files returns correctly the first time, which cuts down on delays before they start. Once a return is submitted, though, tracking it sits with the IRS or the state, not with your preparer. These links get you the same status information their own phone reps see.

<!-- block: checklist-section | variant: with-image | image: federal-refund-tracking.jpg | alt: "Person checking federal tax refund status on a laptop at home" | query: "person checking laptop tax refund" -->
## Federal refund tracking links

The IRS updates refund status once every 24 hours, usually overnight, so refreshing the page five times before lunch won't turn up anything new. E-filed returns typically show a status within 24 hours of acceptance. Paper returns can take four weeks or longer before anything appears at all. Before you click through, have your Social Security number, filing status, and exact refund amount ready. The tool requires all three to pull up your record.

- [IRS Where's My Refund](https://www.irs.gov/refunds), check the status of your current-year federal refund directly through the IRS.
- [Where's My Amended Return](https://www.irs.gov/filing/wheres-my-amended-return), track a Form 1040-X after you've filed an amendment. These can take up to 16 weeks to fully process.

<!-- block: checklist-section | variant: with-image-left | image: utah-state-refund-tracking.jpg | alt: "Utah taxpayer reviewing state tax refund status on a phone" | query: "person reviewing tax documents phone" -->
## Utah state refund tracking links

Utah processes refunds through its Taxpayer Access Point, and the timeline runs separately from the IRS. E-filed Utah returns generally move through the system within a few weeks. Paper filings take longer, since they're reviewed by hand rather than scanned automatically. Utah's status codes also differ from the federal tool's, so don't assume a mismatch between your state and federal status means something went wrong. It usually just means the two agencies are on different clocks.

- [Utah TAP Where's My Refund](https://tax.utah.gov/online-services/wheres-my-refund/), look up your Utah state refund status directly through the Utah State Tax Commission.

## What to do if your refund is delayed or flagged

A refund that's stuck usually comes down to one of three things: identity verification, a math-error notice, or a manual review flag. The IRS sends a letter in each case, and what you do with it matters more than most people realize. Respond the wrong way, or ignore it, and your refund can sit for months instead of weeks. Andrew (Andy) James Stewart, EA, and Peter Williams, EA, handle IRS correspondence for Kinexus clients on a regular basis, drafting responses to notices, walking through identity verification through the correct channel, and following up with the IRS directly rather than leaving it to a client to sit on hold. If a notice landed in your mailbox and the language reads like legal code, that's exactly the situation our audit protection service was built for. You shouldn't have to decode an IRS letter alone to find out what's happening with your own money.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Refund tracker

**Q: How long does it take to get a federal tax refund in Utah?**
A: Most e-filed federal refunds arrive within 21 days of IRS acceptance. Paper returns take four weeks or longer. Refunds involving credits like the Earned Income Tax Credit may be held until mid-February by law, regardless of filing date.

**Q: How do I check my Utah state refund status?**
A: Use the Utah State Tax Commission's Taxpayer Access Point (TAP) Where's My Refund tool online. You'll need your Social Security number and the exact refund amount from your return. Utah's processing timeline runs separately from the IRS and may show different status codes.

**Q: Why is my refund taking longer than expected?**
A: Common causes include identity verification holds, math-error notices, missing forms, or a manual review flag. The IRS or Utah State Tax Commission will typically mail a letter explaining the issue. Kinexus CPAs' EA-credentialed team can review the notice and respond on your behalf.

**Q: What information do I need to check my refund status?**
A: For both federal and Utah state lookups, have your Social Security number (or ITIN), your filing status, and the exact whole-dollar refund amount from your return ready. All three are required for the system to locate your record.
