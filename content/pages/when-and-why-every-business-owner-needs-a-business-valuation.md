## Why the number in your head isn't a real valuation

Most business owners can rattle off a number when asked what their company is worth. Usually it's built from a competitor's sale price, a comment a banker made in passing, or a multiple pulled from an industry rumor. None of that holds up when it actually matters.

A real valuation is built on methodology: adjusted financials, industry benchmarks, cash flow analysis, and defensible assumptions a lender, buyer, or court will accept. The gut-feel number and the defensible number are often nowhere close, and the gap shows up at the worst possible time, mid-negotiation, mid-divorce, or mid-financing application. If you're making a decision that hinges on what your business is worth, guessing is a risk you don't need to take.

## Key moments that trigger the need for a valuation

A formal valuation isn't something you need every year, but there are specific moments where operating without one becomes expensive. Owners typically reach out to Kinexus for a valuation ahead of one of these events:

- Selling the business, merging with another company, or fielding an unsolicited offer
- Bringing on new partners, shareholders, or an ESOP
- Divorce or estate planning where the business is a marital or estate asset
- Drafting or updating a buy-sell agreement between co-owners
- Applying for financing or an SBA loan that requires a documented valuation
- Litigation, shareholder disputes, or IRS matters that require a defensible number

Each of these situations puts real money on the table, and each one is judged by a bank, a court, an ex-spouse's attorney, or a business partner who has no reason to accept your estimate at face value. A CPA-prepared valuation gives you a number that stands up to scrutiny instead of one that starts an argument.

## How proactive valuation supports growth planning, not just transactions

Most firms only bring up valuation when a sale is already on the table. That's backwards. By the time a deal is in motion, there's little room left to improve the number.

Kinexus treats valuation as part of ongoing business management services, the same CFO-level advisory work that tracks your margins, tax position, and entity structure year-round. Knowing what your business is worth today, and what's actually driving or dragging that number, shapes decisions long before a sale: how you price a job, whether to add a location, when to bring on a partner, or how to structure a buy-sell agreement before it's needed. Owners who understand their valuation trajectory make better decisions at every stage, not just the exit. That's a different conversation than a once-a-year tax appointment, and it's the one worth having.

## What goes into an accurate business valuation

A credible valuation pulls from more than a tax return. It starts with normalized financial statements, adjusting for owner compensation, one-time expenses, and related-party transactions that distort the real picture. From there, we look at industry benchmarks and comparable transactions to see how similar businesses have actually sold, not what a rule-of-thumb multiple suggests.

Cash flow gets weighted heavily, since most valuation methods (income, market, or asset-based) ultimately come back to what the business can reliably generate going forward. Tangible and intangible assets, equipment, real estate, customer contracts, goodwill, get accounted for separately. Current market conditions, financing rates, and buyer appetite in your specific sector round out the picture. None of this is guesswork; it's a documented process that produces a number you can defend to a bank, a buyer, or a judge.

## Industry-specific considerations for valuation

A generic valuation approach misses what actually drives value in your industry. Kinexus applies methodology built around how each sector operates.

### Construction
icon: Hammer

Work-in-progress schedules, bonding capacity, and backlog value matter as much as trailing revenue. A contractor's valuation has to account for percentage-of-completion accounting and equipment depreciation.

### Restaurants
icon: Building

Multi-location operators face franchise agreements, lease terms, and labor cost ratios that swing value significantly. Same-store sales trends carry more weight than total revenue alone.

### Medical Professionals
icon: Stethoscope

Practice valuations weigh payer mix, provider-dependent goodwill, and equipment age. A physician-owned practice with heavy owner dependency values differently than a group practice with associate providers.

### Real Estate
icon: Building2

Investment portfolios and rental operations are valued on cash-on-cash return, cap rates, and debt structure, not just assessed property value.

### Service-Based Businesses
icon: Briefcase

Consultants, agencies, and staffing firms are judged on client concentration, recurring revenue, and how much of the business depends on the owner personally.
