## Where new businesses run into trouble without the right foundation

Most startup accounting problems trace back to decisions made in the first six months. The same patterns show up across contractors, restaurant operators, and service-based founders launching new companies in southern Utah:

- Choosing an LLC, S-corp, or sole proprietorship without understanding the tax tradeoffs
- Mixing personal and business funds in one bank account
- Missing an S-corp or entity election that had a hard filing deadline
- Running the business with no real-time view of cash flow or margin
- Doing bookkeeping in spreadsheets that don't reconcile once tax season arrives

None of this means the business is failing. It means the founder was busy building something, which is exactly why the accounting and tax foundation needs to be right before it costs real money to fix.

## Business foundation services to start strong

Some early decisions are hard to unwind once they're made. Entity type, tax elections, and how the books get set up on day one all fall into that category, so getting them right the first time matters more than most founders realize.

### Entity structuring
icon: Scale

LLC, S-corp, partnership, or sole proprietorship each carry different tax and liability outcomes. We help founders pick the structure that fits how the business actually operates, not a generic default.

### EIN and business registration
icon: FileCheck

Federal EIN, state registration, and local licensing in Washington County get handled correctly the first time, so a missed filing doesn't surface as a penalty later.

### Chart of accounts setup
icon: ClipboardCheck

A chart of accounts built for the business's actual revenue streams and cost structure, so the numbers mean something from the first transaction forward.

### Initial tax elections
icon: FileText

S-corp elections, accounting method choices, and other early filings have real deadlines. Missing one can mean waiting a full year to fix it.

## Tax strategy that grows with your business, not just files it

A startup's first tax return looks nothing like its fifth. Revenue grows, entity structure gets revisited, owners start taking distributions, and the tax bill that felt manageable at $150,000 in revenue can look very different past $500,000. Filing a return once a year doesn't catch any of that early enough to plan around it.

Shawn T. Gubler holds the Certified Tax Coach (CTC) credential alongside his CPA and MAcc, and it shows up in how the firm approaches business and personal tax together. Quarterly check-ins, entity reviews, and advanced strategies like retirement plan design or accountable plans get built into the plan before December, not discovered in March. [Business tax planning](/what-we-do/business-tax) and [personal income tax](/what-we-do/personal-income-tax) get coordinated so an owner's business decisions and personal return move in the same direction.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Entrepreneurs and startups

**Q: When should a startup in St. George hire a CPA instead of doing it themselves?**
A: As soon as you're choosing a business entity, hiring your first employee, or bringing in outside revenue, a CPA can prevent costly missteps like wrong entity elections or commingled finances. Kinexus works with founders from the earliest stage, not just at tax time.

**Q: What is included in Business Foundation Services?**
A: Business Foundation Services cover entity structuring, EIN and business registration, chart of accounts setup, and initial tax elections like S-corp timing. These are decisions that are difficult and costly to reverse once made incorrectly.

**Q: Does Kinexus only work with startups, or established businesses too?**
A: Kinexus serves entrepreneurs from initial launch through scaling and profitability, adjusting services as a business grows. Early-stage founders often start with bookkeeping and foundation services, then add tax planning and CFO-level advisory as revenue increases.

**Q: How does year-round tax planning differ from annual tax prep?**
A: Year-round planning means quarterly check-ins and strategy adjustments as your business changes, rather than a single meeting each April. Shawn T. Gubler, CPA, CTC, MAcc, builds tax strategy around a company's real-time financial picture, not last year's return.

**Q: Can Kinexus help with fundraising or getting a business loan?**
A: Yes. Business Management Services include cash flow forecasting and financial reporting that lenders and investors expect to see, helping founders get lending or fundraising ready before they need capital.
