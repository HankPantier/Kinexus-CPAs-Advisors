## Accounting built for contractors, not generic small businesses

Most CPA firms treat a general contractor the same way they treat a retail shop or a dentist's office. That approach falls apart fast once retainage is sitting on your balance sheet, your bonding capacity depends on clean financials, and you won't know if a job actually made money until the punch list is done.

Kinexus CPAs & Advisors works with contractors, subcontractors, and construction business owners across St. George and southern Utah who need more than a firm that shows up in April. We build our process around how construction businesses actually run: job costing by project and phase, work-in-progress schedules that satisfy bonding agents and lenders, and tax and cash flow planning that accounts for retainage instead of ignoring it.

If you've ever handed a banker a P&L that didn't match what you knew was true on the ground, that's the gap we're built to close.

## Job costing and work in progress reporting that keeps projects profitable

A job that looks profitable on paper can bleed cash for months before anyone notices. Most off-the-shelf bookkeeping setups track expenses company-wide, not by project phase, so a contractor can't tell which job is actually making money until it's too late to adjust the bid or the crew schedule.

We build job costing systems that break down labor, materials, subcontractor invoices, and overhead by job and phase, so profitability shows up in real time instead of at tax time. Our [WIP schedules](/industries/construction/wip-schedules-percentage-completion) follow percentage-of-completion methods your bonding agent and lender already expect, updated on a schedule you can actually use when bidding the next job.

Retainage sitting on the balance sheet is its own cash flow problem. We help you [plan around it](/retainage-is-holding-your-cash-hostage-heres-how-to-plan-around-it) instead of discovering the gap when payroll is due.

## Proactive tax planning for construction businesses

Tax prep once a year tells you what you owed. Tax planning, done quarterly, tells you how to owe less. Shawn T. Gubler, CPA, CTC, MAcc leads our tax strategy work with construction clients ranging from single-crew remodelers to multi-entity general contractors bidding public jobs.

- Entity structuring that separates liability and improves tax efficiency across bonding, equipment, and operating entities
- Section 179 and bonus depreciation planning timed around equipment purchases, not filed after the fact
- Multi-entity structuring for larger contractors running separate operating and equipment companies
- Quarterly estimated tax planning tied to your actual project pipeline instead of last year's revenue

Our [advanced tax planning](/services/advanced-tax-planning) and [entity structuring](/services/entity-structuring) work happens on a calendar built around your bid schedule, not the April deadline.

## CFO-level insight for scaling contractors

Taking on a bigger project changes everything: bonding requirements, equipment financing, crew size, cash flow timing. A contractor who was fine running the numbers themselves at $2 million in revenue often isn't equipped for the same job at $8 million.

Our [business management services](/what-we-do/business-management-services) give growing contractors CFO-level insight without a CFO salary: cash flow forecasting tied to your project schedule, bonding capacity analysis before you bid the next job, and margin reporting by project type so you know which kind of work actually pays.

RJ Mathews, CPA, and Chase Gubler, CPA, MAcc, work directly with contractor clients on these decisions, translating the numbers into a plan you can act on before the bid deadline, not after the job is already underway.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Construction accounting

**Q: What makes construction accounting different from regular small business accounting?**
A: Construction businesses deal with job costing by project, retainage held back on invoices, WIP schedules for bonding, and revenue that doesn't match cash timing. Generic bookkeeping setups can't track profitability by job, which is why contractors need industry-specific accounting support.

**Q: Do you prepare WIP schedules for bonding and lending purposes?**
A: Yes. We build percentage-of-completion WIP schedules updated on a regular cadence, not just once a year, so bonding agents and lenders receive numbers that reflect your actual project status when you need financing or bonding capacity.

**Q: Can you handle certified payroll for prevailing wage jobs?**
A: Yes, our payroll team manages certified payroll and prevailing wage reporting for contractors working public or union jobs, along with subcontractor 1099 tracking, so filings are accurate and on time throughout the year.

**Q: How is tax planning different from tax preparation for contractors?**
A: Tax preparation reports what already happened. Our tax planning, led by Shawn T. Gubler, CPA, CTC, MAcc, works year-round on entity structuring, Section 179 equipment planning, and quarterly estimates so contractors reduce their tax bill before it's due, not after.
