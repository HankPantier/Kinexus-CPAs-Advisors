## Tax planning tips for growing businesses

Multi-entity structures, high-income years, and asset sales all change the math on what you owe. These tips come from work our CPAs, including Shawn T. Gubler, CPA, CTC, MAcc, do year-round with business owners deciding how to structure, sell, or grow.

### Choosing the right entity before year-end
photo: entity-structure-review.jpg
query: business owner reviewing legal documents

An S corp, LLC, or partnership can mean very different tax bills for the same business. If you haven't revisited your entity choice since you started, this is worth a look before December 31.
[Read more](/is-your-business-structure-costing-you-money)

### What related-entity transactions mean for your tax bill
photo: related-entity-transactions.jpg
query: two business owners discussing contract

If you run more than one business, or lease property to your own company, the IRS expects those transactions to be priced like they'd be between strangers. Get this wrong and an audit gets expensive fast.
[Read more](/transfer-pricing-and-related-entities-what-business-owners-need-to-know)

### Knowing your business's real value before you need it
photo: business-valuation-meeting.jpg
query: accountant presenting financial charts

A valuation isn't just for a sale. Buy-sell agreements, estate planning, and partner buyouts all hinge on a number you should already know.
[Read more](/when-and-why-every-business-owner-needs-a-business-valuation)

## Bookkeeping and back-office tips for small business owners

Most owners start out doing their own books, and most outgrow it faster than they realize. These tips mark the point where spreadsheets stop being enough.

- Reconcile bank accounts monthly, not quarterly. Errors compound the longer they sit.
- Separate business and personal accounts on day one, not after your first tax season.
- Move to zero-based budgeting when inflation makes last year's budget meaningless. [See how it works](/zero-based-budgeting-a-smarter-way-to-manage-your-money-during-persistent-inflation)
- Track job costs or project margins in real time, not at year-end, if you bid or quote work.
- Know the difference between bookkeeping and outsourced accounting before you outgrow a part-time hire. Our [bookkeeping](/what-we-do/bookkeeping) and [outsourced accounting](/what-we-do/outsourced-accounting) services pick up exactly where DIY stops working.

## Industry-specific insights for construction, restaurants, and real estate

Generic tax advice misses the details that actually move the needle for contractors, restaurant operators, and property investors. These tips come from the same industry teams that built our [construction](/who-we-serve/construction), [restaurant](/who-we-serve/restaurants), and [real estate](/who-we-serve/real-estate) practices.

### Construction
icon: Hammer

Retainage sitting on your books isn't cash you can spend, and a WIP schedule that doesn't match your bank's expectations can stall a bonding decision. Two reads worth your time: [why retainage holds your cash hostage](/retainage-is-holding-your-cash-hostage-heres-how-to-plan-around-it) and [what your WIP schedule tells your banker](/what-a-wip-schedule-actually-tells-you-and-why-your-banker-cares).

### Restaurants
icon: Users

A second location changes your tax exposure, not just your payroll. Multi-location and franchise operators need a state-by-state strategy before they sign a second lease. [See our multi-location tax approach](/industries/restaurants/multi-location-franchise-tax-strategy).

### Real estate
icon: Building

Depreciation schedules, 1031 exchanges, and cost segregation studies only pay off if they're planned before the deal closes, not after. Investors working with our real estate team plan the tax outcome alongside the purchase.

## Navigating IRS notices and audit concerns

An IRS letter in the mail doesn't have to mean a fire drill. Andrew (Andy) James Stewart, EA, and Peter Williams, EA, MAcc, handle notices and audit correspondence directly with the IRS, so clients aren't left guessing what a CP2000 or an audit request actually means for them.

The tip that matters most here: respond to a notice within the deadline printed on the letter, even if you're not sure what it means yet. Missing that window narrows your options. Our [IRS representation and tax resolution](/services/irs-representation-tax-resolution) service and [audit protection](/what-we-do/audit-protection) plan exist so a notice gets handled the day it arrives, not the week before the deadline.
