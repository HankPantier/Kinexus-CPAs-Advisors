## A CPA partner who understands nonprofit accounting

Running a nonprofit in Washington County means stretching every dollar toward mission, not overhead. Kinexus CPAs & Advisors works with local nonprofits on accounting, tax filings, and financial strategy built around fund accounting rules, not generic small-business bookkeeping repackaged for a 501(c)(3). Shawn T. Gubler, CPA, CTC, MAcc, and the team at Kinexus understand that a nonprofit's books answer to a board, a grantor, and the IRS all at once.

Most accounting firms treat nonprofit work as a side service. Kinexus treats it as a distinct discipline, with staff who know the difference between restricted and unrestricted funds and can explain that difference to a board member who isn't an accountant. That's the kind of clarity that keeps grant funding intact and audits uneventful.

## Challenges nonprofits face with limited financial resources

A lean nonprofit staff rarely includes a full-time controller, and that gap shows up fast. Grant reporting deadlines pile up, board members ask for financials in a format that actually makes sense, and someone still has to keep restricted and unrestricted funds separated correctly on paper, not just in spirit.

Common pressure points Kinexus sees among nonprofit clients in Washington County:

- Tracking multiple grants and donor restrictions without dedicated fund accounting software support
- Producing board-ready financial reports on a monthly or quarterly cadence
- Preparing for a financial statement audit required by a major grantor
- Managing payroll and benefits with one or two administrative staff wearing every hat
- Maintaining internal controls that satisfy both the board and outside auditors

An outsourced accounting relationship solves this without adding a full-time salary to the budget.

## Bookkeeping and accounting built for fund accounting needs

Donor dollars come with conditions, and a general ledger built for a retail shop won't track them correctly. Kinexus sets up bookkeeping and outsourced accounting around program-based and grant-based reporting, so a director can tell a funder exactly how their money was spent, down to the program line item.

Monthly close, expense classification, and reconciliation happen through a modern, secure digital process, which means board treasurers get clean statements without chasing down a shoebox of receipts. RJ Mathews, CPA, and Morgan Nethercott, CPA, both work directly with clients who need audit-ready books, not just a checkbook register that balances.

The result is transparency a board can stand behind and a paper trail that holds up when a grantor or auditor asks questions.

## Form 990 preparation and nonprofit tax compliance

Losing tax-exempt status over a missed or mishandled Form 990 is a real risk, not a hypothetical one. Kinexus prepares Form 990, 990-EZ, and 990-T filings with attention to the disclosures that protect exempt status, including unrelated business income, executive compensation reporting, and public support test calculations that keep a public charity classified correctly.

This isn't a once-a-year filing errand. Andy Stewart, EA, and Peter Williams, EA, MAcc, monitor compliance changes throughout the year so a nonprofit isn't caught off guard by a new reporting threshold or a state registration renewal. That proactive approach matters more for nonprofits than most businesses, since a compliance misstep can threaten the exemption itself. For organizations facing an IRS notice or inquiry, Kinexus also provides [audit protection](/what-we-do/audit-protection) and [IRS representation](/services/irs-representation-tax-resolution) support.

## CFO-level guidance for growing nonprofit organizations

A nonprofit that's outgrown its founding budget needs more than compliance work. It needs someone who can translate financial statements into a decision about whether to apply for a new grant, hire a program manager, or hold off another year. That's what Business Management Services is built for.

Chase Gubler, CPA, MAcc, works with executive directors and boards on budgeting, cash flow forecasting, and sustainability planning tied directly to grant cycles and fundraising calendars. Instead of reviewing financials once a year at the annual meeting, boards get ongoing insight into where the organization stands and what decisions are coming next quarter, not just what already happened last quarter.
