## Accounting challenges unique to real estate

Real estate accounting rarely stays simple past the second property. Each acquisition raises a new question: hold it in an existing LLC, form a new entity, or roll it into a series structure? Get the entity setup wrong and you end up with commingled liability, messier books, and a harder conversation with your lender.

Depreciation adds another layer. Rental property depreciation, cost segregation opportunities, and bonus depreciation rules shift the math on every property differently, and missing a component-level breakdown can leave real deductions unclaimed. Add passive activity loss limitations, the real estate professional status test, and the timing rules around 1031 exchanges, and it's easy to see why generic tax prep leaves money on the table.

Kinexus works through these details before the deal closes, not after the return is filed. That includes mapping out how a 1031 exchange affects your basis, whether a cost segregation study makes sense for a recent purchase, and how passive activity rules apply to your specific mix of properties and involvement.

## Proactive tax strategy for property owners and investors

Shawn T. Gubler holds the Certified Tax Coach (CTC) designation, a credential built specifically around advanced, forward-looking tax planning rather than compliance-only preparation. That distinction matters for real estate clients, where the biggest savings usually come from decisions made months before a return is ever filed.

Depreciation strategy is a good example. A cost segregation study on a recently acquired rental or commercial property can accelerate deductions into the years you need them most, but only if it's planned before year-end, not discovered during filing season. The same goes for 1031 exchanges: the identification and closing windows are unforgiving, and the tax outcome depends on decisions made well before the exchange agreement is signed.

Entity structuring works the same way. Whether a new acquisition belongs in an existing LLC, a new one, or a holding company arrangement changes your liability exposure and your tax picture at the same time. Kinexus builds these conversations into regular check-ins throughout the year, so the strategy is set before the transaction closes, not reconstructed after the fact.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Real estate accounting

**Q: Does Kinexus work with real estate investors outside St. George?**
A: Yes. While the firm is based in St. George, Utah, Kinexus works with rental property owners and investors managing portfolios across multiple states, using a secure digital portal for document sharing, e-signatures, and scheduled video check-ins.

**Q: Can Kinexus help with a 1031 exchange?**
A: Yes. Kinexus plans 1031 exchange timing, identification requirements, and basis calculations before the exchange agreement is signed, working alongside your qualified intermediary to keep the transaction inside IRS deadlines.

**Q: Do I need a separate LLC for each rental property?**
A: It depends on your liability exposure, lender requirements, and portfolio size. Kinexus reviews entity structuring for each new acquisition, weighing an existing LLC, a new entity, or a holding company arrangement against your specific situation.

**Q: What is a cost segregation study and do I need one?**
A: A cost segregation study breaks a property into components that depreciate faster than the building itself, accelerating deductions. It typically makes sense for recently purchased or renovated properties above a certain value, which Kinexus can evaluate during tax planning.
