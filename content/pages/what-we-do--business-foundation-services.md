## Start your business on solid financial ground

Starting a business means dozens of decisions before you've earned your first dollar: entity type, EIN, bank accounts, bookkeeping software, tax elections, licensing. Get one wrong and you're paying for it later in penalties, missed deductions, or a structure that costs you thousands as you grow.

Most accountants show up once a year, in March, with a shoebox of receipts to sort through. Kinexus works differently. Shawn T. Gubler, CPA, CTC, MAcc, and the team build your financial foundation from the day you form your entity, not after your first tax return is due. The accounting system, tax structure, and compliance calendar get set up correctly the first time, so you spend your early months building the business instead of untangling paperwork.

<!-- block: checklist-section | variant: with-image | image: startup-checklist-review.jpg | alt: "Entrepreneur reviewing a business startup checklist with an advisor" | query: "entrepreneur reviewing startup checklist advisor" -->
## Key startup decisions handled from day one

New business owners face a stack of foundational decisions in the first 90 days. Here's what we help you sort through before they turn into costly mistakes:

- Entity selection: LLC, S-corp, C-corp, partnership, or sole proprietorship, matched to your liability exposure and tax goals
- EIN and state registration: getting your business properly registered with the IRS and the Utah Division of Corporations
- Accounting system setup: choosing and configuring software, such as QuickBooks Online, that scales as revenue grows
- Tax election strategy: timing an S-corp election, choosing a fiscal year, and setting up estimated payments correctly from the start
- Initial compliance requirements: business licenses, sales tax registration, payroll setup, and recordkeeping standards that hold up under a lender or IRS review

Miss one of these early, and you often can't fix it retroactively. That's why the sequence matters as much as the individual decisions.

<!-- block: content-split | variant: image-right | image: entity-structure-planning-meeting.jpg | alt: "CPA and business owner discussing entity structure options at a desk" | query: "business owners reviewing entity structure documents" -->
## Entity structuring built for growth, not just launch

The entity that makes sense at launch isn't always the entity that makes sense at $2 million in revenue. Chase Gubler, CPA, MAcc, and RJ Mathews, CPA, work with entrepreneurs to structure entities for where the business is headed, not where it started.

A single-member LLC might be the right call in year one. Once you're hiring employees, bringing on partners, or holding real estate alongside operations, that structure often needs to change. We look at multi-entity setups, holding companies, and the timing of S-corp elections as revenue climbs, and we revisit the structure at each stage instead of leaving it set on autopilot. Full entity structuring support is available through our [entity structuring service](/services/entity-structuring).

## Bookkeeping and financial systems from day one

Most first-time owners start with a spreadsheet or a shoebox, and there's nothing wrong with that in month one. The problem shows up in month twelve, when a bank wants financials for a loan, or an investor asks for a P&L, and none of it reconciles.

Peter Williams, EA, MAcc, and Morgan Nethercott, CPA, help new business owners set up a bookkeeping system built to scale: transactions categorized correctly, accounts reconciled monthly, and reports ready to hand a lender or investor without a scramble. Once the foundation is in place, it connects directly into our ongoing [bookkeeping](/what-we-do/bookkeeping) and [outsourced accounting](/what-we-do/outsourced-accounting) services, so you're not rebuilding your books a year in.

## Proactive tax planning starting at formation

Tax strategy shouldn't start the year you file your first return. It should start the day you choose your entity. Shawn T. Gubler, CPA, CTC (Certified Tax Coach), MAcc, builds tax planning into the formation process itself: timing an S-corp election, structuring owner compensation, and setting up retirement plan options before the deadlines that make them possible have passed.

New business owners who wait until tax season to think about strategy usually miss the elections and deductions that only work if they're arranged in advance. Foundation-stage planning connects directly into our year-round [advanced tax planning](/services/advanced-tax-planning) and [business tax](/what-we-do/business-tax) services, so the strategy set up in year one keeps paying off in year five.

**Q: How do I know which business entity is right for my startup?**
A: The right entity depends on liability exposure, expected revenue, and long-term plans like adding partners or real estate. Chase Gubler, CPA, MAcc, and RJ Mathews, CPA, review your specific situation and recommend an LLC, S-corp, or other structure, then revisit it as your business grows.
