## When your current structure stops serving your growth

Most entity decisions get made once, in the excitement of opening day, and then never get revisited. That works fine until it doesn't. A construction company that started as a single-member LLC and now runs three crews and $2 million in annual revenue faces different liability exposure and tax brackets than the day it formed. A medical practice adding a second physician-owner needs a structure that handles distributions, buy-in, and liability differently than a solo practitioner setup.

Certain triggers should prompt a structure review: crossing into a new tax bracket, bringing on a business partner, acquiring rental property or a second location, or separating operations from real estate holdings. If any of these sound familiar, your entity paperwork may be costing you money or exposing you to risk it was never built to handle.

## Choosing between LLCs, S-corps, and multi-entity setups

An LLC taxed as a sole proprietorship or partnership is simple to maintain but leaves self-employment tax exposure on your full share of profit. Elect S-corp treatment once profits justify it, and you can split income between salary and distributions, often reducing that tax bite. An S-corp comes with payroll requirements, reasonable-compensation rules, and stricter ownership limits that don't fit every business.

For owners running more than one line of business, or holding real estate alongside operations, a single entity often isn't the right answer at all. The choice isn't LLC versus S-corp in isolation. It's which combination protects your assets, minimizes your combined tax bill, and still lets you run the business without unnecessary complexity.

Questions worth answering before you file anything:

- Self-employment tax exposure under a default LLC
- Payroll and reasonable-compensation requirements once an S-corp election makes sense
- Liability separation between operating entities and asset-holding entities
- State filing, franchise tax, and registered agent differences across structures

## Structuring for multiple entities, holding companies, and real estate

Owners running more than one business, or layering real estate into their holdings, need structuring that goes beyond a single LLC filing. Shawn Gubler, CPA, CTC, MAcc, and Chase Gubler, CPA, MAcc, work through multi-entity structures where an operating company handles day-to-day business and a separate holding company owns the real estate, equipment, or intellectual property leased back to it.

This separation matters for two reasons: liability and tax. If a slip-and-fall claim hits your restaurant's operating entity, the building and equipment sitting in a separate holding LLC stay protected. On the tax side, a holding company structure can create rental deductions, depreciation planning, and cleaner books when it's time to sell one piece of the business without unwinding the whole thing.

Real estate investors with multiple properties face a related question: one LLC per property, a series LLC, or a portfolio-level entity. The right answer depends on financing needs, the number of properties, and how actively you're acquiring.

## How entity structure connects to your tax strategy

Entity structure isn't a form you file once and forget. It's one lever in a tax plan that gets reviewed every year, not just when a return is due. Shawn Gubler holds the Certified Tax Coach (CTC) credential specifically because tax planning built around a client's structure, timing, and elections produces bigger savings than year-end scrambling ever will.

An S-corp election that made sense at $80,000 in profit may cost you money at $400,000, once payroll taxes, retirement plan limits, and qualified business income deductions shift the math. A multi-entity setup built for liability protection five years ago might now be creating unnecessary complexity if one of those entities sits dormant.

Entity structure gets reviewed as part of ongoing tax planning, not as a stand-alone project. That means catching the moment your structure needs an update before it shows up as a bigger tax bill next April.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Entity structuring for growing businesses

**Q: When should a growing business review its entity structure?**
A: Review your structure when you cross into a new tax bracket, bring on a partner, acquire rental property, add a second location, or separate real estate from operations. These triggers often mean your current LLC or S-corp setup no longer matches your liability and tax situation.

**Q: Is an S-corp always better than an LLC for tax savings?**
A: Not always. An S-corp can reduce self-employment tax once profits are high enough to justify payroll and reasonable-compensation requirements, but it adds administrative complexity. The right choice depends on your profit level, ownership structure, and long-term plans.

**Q: Do I need a separate holding company for my real estate?**
A: If you own the building your business operates from, or multiple rental properties, a separate holding company can protect those assets from operating liability and create cleaner tax and depreciation planning. Kinexus structures operating and holding entities together as part of one strategy.

**Q: Does Kinexus handle the legal filing for a new entity?**
A: Kinexus coordinates directly with your attorney, or refers one, to handle formation documents and operating agreements. The CPA team focuses on the tax strategy, financial modeling, and implementation so the legal work and tax plan match.
